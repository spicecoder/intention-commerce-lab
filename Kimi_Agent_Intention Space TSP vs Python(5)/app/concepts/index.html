CONCEPTS / A LAYERED REFERENCE

A vocabulary for situated communication.

Begin with purpose and observation. Move through bounded computation and human participation. Then widen cooperation without silently widening meaning or authority.

ON THIS PAGEC1 · CommunicationC2 · Meaning and occurrenceC3 · Bounded executionC4 · Human participationC5 · Governed cooperation
CHAPTER C1 / COMMUNICATION

Purpose and observation form a Signal.

Communication is not fulfilled merely because fields arrive. The acting context must also retain why those fields were communicated.

Pulse
A normalized, situated observation: {name, Trivalence (Y/U/N), R}. Its name identifies an attention point; R carries Response data.
Intention
The declared purpose under which a set of observations participates. Observation alone does not imply an action.
Signal
An Intention plus a set of Pulses. Exact Signal contracts preserve purpose and observation identities across participation boundaries.
Resolution
A consequential exchange returns a resolution Pulse. Y means resolved, U unresolved or awaiting observation, and N a definite conflict.

Context changes identity

The same name and Trivalence in different contexts do not automatically denote the same Pulse. A governing Space and version are therefore part of situated meaning, not incidental deployment metadata.

Transport success is not business resolution.

A service may return HTTP 200 while correctly recording an inventory rejection. The successful recording must not convert that business N into Y.

CHAPTER C2 / MEANING AND OCCURRENCE

An address is not the value at that address.

Canonical Intention and Pulse/TV identities provide shared semantic addresses. Provenance and Field policy distinguish concrete Response-bearing occurrences.

For example, customer_profile + Y identifies the kind of observation meant. It does not, by itself, select which customer-profile occurrence, source event, or revision should be used in a later activation.

Semantic identity
The declared Intention and Pulse/TV identities. Response data does not redefine what kind of Signal it is.
Concrete occurrence
A particular Response-bearing Signal occurrence with a source, execution locus, provenance, and revision.
Field selection
Explicit policy for selecting among occurrences that share a semantic address. Selection is not silent rewriting.

Continuity without collapse

A later participation may relate a new purpose to earlier observations under declared matching, recurrence, and completion policies. The immutable earlier Signal is not rewritten to manufacture continuity.

CHAPTER C3 / BOUNDED EXECUTION

A DN acts inside a configured participation.

The public DN contract is one designated input Signal and one designated output Signal per activation. Runtime machinery decides when that participation is ready and where its output may go.

S1→IC / DN→designated S2
Design Neuron / DN
The computational hub. Only DN computation may change Pulse Responses; changes leave through the declared output Signal.
Intention Container / IC
Locates one DN behaviour in a context, pairing an absorbed S1 with a designated S2 and declaring available API, display, absorb, and error ports.
CPUX
A semantic and coordination perimeter with a shared Field. It is a runtime boundary, not automatically an Agent or an Intention Space.
Field / FIS / FPS
The Field records purposes in FIS and observations with Responses in FPS. IC readiness tests relate that state to the configured participation.
FieldBoard
Routes complete committed Signal occurrences between CPUXs and preserves the continuing execution locus. It does not own each CPUX’s Field semantics.
Readiness and claim

The runtime briefly commits Field admission and the readiness claim before invoking a DN. A DN may then perform its own atomic business-data transaction. Its output Signals express the after-effect of that work.

Direct display versus designated output

A direct-display Signal informs GridLookout. The designated S2 continues cooperation through the IC. The two paths must not be conflated.

Prototype-first conformance

A stub can satisfy the same S1–S2 contract for early development and tests. This validates interface mechanics and declared Y/U/N behavior; it does not establish the correctness of a later real-service binding.

CHAPTER C4 / HUMAN PARTICIPATION

A common perceptual surface. Distinct authority.

GridLookout makes human interaction, data exchange, and provenance uniform across devices and implementation technologies. Taskpoints give human action a declared boundary without treating presentation as permission.

GridLookout
Projects App-emit Signal occurrences into a human-facing perceptual bound. It provides a common interaction surface across coding languages and platforms. The rendered surface is a projection, not the underlying execution record.
Perceptual bound
The human-perceivable unit of interaction that holds a situation’s purpose, relevant data, available participation, and resolution together. It defines interaction scope, not computational or effect authority.
Cross-device uniformity
A shared perceptual, data-exchange, and provenance contract across renderers. It does not require pixel-identical interfaces: layout, input method, and accessibility may adapt while the interaction identities and records remain consistent.
Taskpoint
A declared point within the participation surface at which a person may construct and release a complete App-absorb Signal with the required Intention and Pulses.
Lifecycle control
A direct runtime request, such as termination, remains distinct from semantic Taskpoint traffic.

Provenance can connect what was presented, which data crossed the perceptual bound, who acted, and which resolution followed. Conversational wording is not automatically authorization. A consequential act requires its declared Taskpoint participation and effect authority.

CHAPTER C5 / GOVERNED COOPERATION

Cooperation is more than shared registration.

An architecture can support wider participation only if it also states which meanings, authorities, mappings, and resolutions govern each crossing.

Agent
A named, bounded composition of conformant DNs, explicit Signal paths, authority, policy, resolutions, and provenance. Multiple DNs or LLM personas do not automatically constitute multiple agents.
Intention Space
A versioned semantic boundary for situated activation. A DN may participate in many Spaces; each activation has exactly one governing Space and version.
Intention Colosseum
Governed cooperation among exact member Space versions through explicit crossing rules, tests, authority, and provenance.
C–MAP
A declared cross-Space Signal mapping. It may project, copy, or rename declared envelopes, but cannot hide a value-changing transformation or turn transport success into business Y.
Intention Universe
Addressable coexistence of Colosseums without universal trust, interoperability, authority, or a global resolver.
Value conversion requires computation.

If a crossing converts units, calculates a new value, or changes business meaning, that transformation belongs in a DN. A C–MAP must not conceal it.
